Chapter One

"The city didn't just change what it ate.
It changed how it thought about growing."

By the 2030s, Davis had become the first city in California to reach food-system carbon neutrality. Vertical gardens climbed building facades. Every restaurant sourced within thirty miles. The question was no longer what to eat — but how to eat in relationship with the land around you.

The Systems

Six ways the city
feeds itself differently

Each practice woven into daily life — not as policy, but as habit.

🌿

Vertical Farming

Forty-story towers produce leafy greens year-round with 95% less water than conventional fields.

🔄

Closed-Loop Composting

Food scraps return to soil within 72 hours — kitchen to garden, week after week, nothing wasted.

☀️

Solar Kitchen Energy

Rooftop arrays power every stove and refrigerator. Summer surplus stored for winter menus.

💧

Water Reclamation

Greywater filters through constructed wetlands. Each litre of cooking water used three times over.

🍂

Seasonal Menus Only

Every menu turns with the valley — a living calendar of what the land is ready to offer this week.

📦

Zero-Waste Packaging

Containers grown from mycelium. Utensils pressed from rice husks. Packaging as alive as the meal.

A Morning in 2045

Mia wakes to the smell
of her building's garden

On the fourth floor of the Solstice Residences, Mia Chen steps onto her balcony and reaches through the vertical garden panel to pick three leaves of fresh basil. The dew is still on them.

Downstairs, the shared kitchen is already humming. Solar panels captured enough overnight for a full day of cooking. Neighbours share the harvest, swap seedlings, and leave notes about what's ready.

Tonight she's going to the Living Table — where the menu changes every day based on what arrived from the surrounding farms that morning.
