Chapter One

"The city didn't just change what it ate.
It changed how it thought about growing."

By the 2030s, Davis had become the first city in California to reach food-system carbon neutrality. Vertical gardens climbed building facades. Every restaurant sourced within thirty miles. The question was no longer what to eat — but how to eat in relationship with the land around you.

The Systems

Six ways the city
feeds itself differently

Each practice woven into daily life — not as policy, but as habit.

Vertical Farming

Forty-story growing towers line the downtown corridor — leafy greens, herbs, and microgreens, year-round, with 95% less water than conventional fields.

Closed-Loop Composting

Every restaurant is paired with a neighborhood hub. Food scraps return to soil within 72 hours — kitchen to garden, week after week, nothing wasted.

Solar Kitchen Energy

Rooftop arrays and community microgrids power every stove and refrigerator. Summer harvest surplus is stored for winter menus — the sun cooks twice.

Water Reclamation

Greywater from every kitchen filters through constructed wetlands before irrigating the city gardens. Each litre of cooking water is used three times.

Seasonal Menus Only

No strawberries in December. No asparagus in August. Every menu turns with the valley — a living calendar of what the land is ready to offer this week.

Zero-Waste Packaging

Containers grown from mycelium. Utensils pressed from rice husks. In Davis 2045, the packaging you hold is as alive as the meal inside it.

A Morning in 2045

Mia wakes to the smell
of her building's garden

On the fourth floor of the Solstice Residences, Mia Chen steps onto her balcony and reaches through the vertical garden panel to pick three leaves of fresh basil. The dew is still on them.

Downstairs, the shared kitchen is already humming. Solar panels captured enough overnight for a full day of cooking. Neighbours share the harvest, swap seedlings, and leave notes about what's ready.

Tonight she's going to the Living Table: the city's beloved community restaurant, where the menu changes every day based on what arrived from the surrounding farms that morning.
