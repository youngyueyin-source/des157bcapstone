Annotated Bibliography

This research supports my capstone project focused on designing a future greenhouse-style restaurant that integrates sustainability, cultural perspectives, and nature-centered design.

Biophilic Design

Kellert, Stephen R., et al. The Practice of Biophilic Design. Terrapin Bright Green, 2015.

This source is about biophilic design, which basically means designing spaces that bring people closer to nature. It talks about things like natural light, plants, water, and organic materials, and how those elements can actually improve people’s mood and well-being. This connects a lot to my project since I’m imagining a greenhouse-style restaurant in the future, and I want it to feel calming and not artificial or overwhelming. What I found helpful is that it doesn’t just say “add plants,” it explains how to design with nature in a more intentional way. It made me realize that my idea could go beyond just looking pretty and actually affect how people feel in the space.

Cultural Perspectives on Nature (Japanese Design)

Walker, Sophie. The Japanese Garden. Phaidon Press, 2017.

This book looks at how Japanese gardens are designed and the philosophy behind them. It focuses a lot on balance, simplicity, and respecting nature instead of trying to control it. I liked how it explained that even small details, like the placement of rocks or water, are meant to create a certain feeling. This is relevant to my project because I want to include cultural ways of thinking about nature, especially from Asian perspectives. Compared to a lot of Western design, this approach feels more peaceful and intentional, which is the kind of vibe I want for my space. This source helped me think about designing an experience, not just a visual environment.

Cultural Perspectives on Sustainability (Indigenous Knowledge)

Kimmerer, Robin Wall. Braiding Sweetgrass: Indigenous Wisdom, Scientific Knowledge, and the Teachings of Plants. Milkweed Editions, 2013.

This book talks about the relationship between people and nature through both Indigenous knowledge and science. One of the main ideas is that humans should have a reciprocal relationship with the environment, meaning we shouldn’t just take from it, but also give back. This stood out to me because a lot of “sustainable design” today still feels kind of surface-level. For my project, I want the future to feel genuinely thoughtful and not just aesthetic, and this book helped shift how I think about that. The author’s background as both a scientist and a member of the Citizen Potawatomi Nation adds a lot of credibility. Overall, it made me think about sustainability as more of a mindset and value system, not just a design feature.

Future of Food + Restaurants

Barber, Dan. The Third Plate: Field Notes on the Future of Food. Penguin Books, 2014.

This book is about rethinking the way we eat and how food systems work. Instead of designing food around what people want, Barber argues that we should design meals based on what can be grown sustainably. This really connects to my idea of a future greenhouse restaurant with vertical farming and seasonal ingredients. I thought it was interesting how food can be part of a larger environmental system, instead of being separate from it. This helps make my project feel more realistic and grounded, not just conceptual. It also gave me ideas for how the dining experience could feel more connected to nature, like eating what’s grown directly in the space.
