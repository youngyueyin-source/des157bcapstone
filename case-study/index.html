The Living Table

A speculative interactive dining experience that imagines a more sustainable food future for Davis, California.

Project Overview

The Living Table is an interactive web experience that explores what sustainable dining could look like in Davis in the year 2045. The project uses storytelling, visual design, and user interaction to help visitors imagine a future where food is local, seasonal, low-waste, and connected to the land around them.

The experience begins with a narrative introduction, then guides users through sustainability concepts such as vertical farming, composting, solar-powered kitchens, water reuse, seasonal eating, and zero-waste packaging. The final interaction allows users to build their own future meal by selecting ingredients from different categories.

My goal was to make sustainability feel less like a lecture and more like a world the user could enter. I wanted the design to feel warm, hopeful, and immersive while still teaching users about food systems and environmental responsibility.

Project Research

My research focused on sustainable food systems, local sourcing, composting, plant-forward eating, and future dining practices. Since the project is set in Davis, I wanted the concept to feel connected to the region’s agricultural identity and environmental values.

I also looked at how interactive websites can make educational content more engaging. Instead of only presenting information through paragraphs, I used scroll-based sections, cards, and a meal-building interaction to help users learn through movement and choice.

Visual Design

The visual direction uses deep greens, soft creams, warm golds, and plant-inspired details to create a calm but futuristic atmosphere. I wanted the site to feel organic rather than technological, so I avoided harsh neon colors and used natural textures, rounded cards, glowing overlays, and floating leaf animations.

The typography and layout were designed to feel editorial and story-driven. Large serif headings give the project a narrative feeling, while simple body text keeps the information easy to read. The design choices support the main theme: a future where technology and nature work together.

User Interface

The interface is organized as a guided journey. Users first enter the world through the hero section, then scroll through the story and sustainability systems before reaching the meal builder. This structure helps the user understand the concept before interacting with it.

The meal builder is the main interactive feature. Users choose one item from each category: vegetable, protein, grain, and cooking method. Their choices appear in a sticky preview bar, and once all categories are complete, they can reveal their final dish.

Important interface features include:

  1. Scroll reveal animations to create pacing.
  2. Clickable ingredient cards with selected states.
  3. A sticky meal preview bar for feedback.
  4. A final overlay showing the generated meal and sustainability score.

Revisions

After testing and reviewing the project, I made changes to improve clarity and usability. I adjusted the navigation flow, simplified parts of the interaction, and made the meal-building process easier to understand. I also refined spacing, button visibility, overlay layout, and the visual hierarchy of the sustainability sections.

One important revision was making the final overlay more readable. Since the overlay contains the dish title, description, score, stats, and buttons, I had to adjust spacing so users could see the full result without the content feeling cramped or cut off.

Summary

This project helped me think about how interaction design can make sustainability feel more personal and engaging. Instead of only telling users what sustainable dining is, the site invites them to participate in a small version of it by building their own future meal.

The biggest challenge was balancing storytelling, research, and interaction without making the page feel overwhelming. Through revisions, I learned how important spacing, visual hierarchy, and feedback are when guiding users through an interactive experience.

Overall, The Living Table became a hopeful vision of a future food system where design helps people connect more thoughtfully with what they eat, where it comes from, and how it affects the world around them.
